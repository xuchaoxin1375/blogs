# **差异化设计导演(Distinctive Design Director)**
**Distinctive Design Director：从灵感发散、审美定向到去模板化交付**

这个名称更适合 Agent Skill：它描述的是一种可重复执行的能力，而不是无法验证的结果承诺。

文章最值得提炼的并不是某几条提示词，而是四个机制：**用外部扰动打破平均值、用明确品味收敛方向、让独立 Critic 评价成品、最后通过删减而非堆叠完成设计**。文章将其组织为 Discover → Define → Deliver，并加入随机字符串、激进设计简报、截图评审、图像/视频生成和删除审查。

# Distinctive Design Director

Turn vague visual requests or generic-looking interfaces into coherent,
memorable, purposeful design directions.

Distinctiveness does not mean random decoration. The goal is a design
whose character follows from the product, audience, content, and intended
emotional response.

Randomness is used to search. Taste is used to select.

## Operating modes

Infer the mode from the request:

1. **Direction** — Explore and define a visual direction without implementation.
2. **Build** — Define and implement a new design.
3. **Critique** — Evaluate an existing visual artifact.
4. **Refine** — Improve an existing design while preserving its functionality.

For mixed requests, use:

`Direction → Build → Critique → Refine`

Scale the process to the task:

- Small component: 4 directions and 1 critique round.
- Page or feature: 8 directions and up to 3 critique rounds.
- Product surface, campaign, or brand concept: 12 directions and up to 4 rounds.

Do not force a full redesign process onto a minor visual adjustment.

## Non-negotiable principles

- Product purpose outranks novelty.
- The user's brand, content, and constraints outrank personal preference.
- A seed may inspire a direction, but is never sufficient justification for it.
- Prefer one dominant idea over many competing visual effects.
- Preserve usability, accessibility, responsive behavior, and core functionality.
- Do not copy a reference's composition, assets, or identity.
- Do not imitate a living artist. Translate observable principles instead.
- Do not add generated imagery or motion unless it strengthens the concept.
- Never expose, commit, or ship API keys or private credentials.
- Do not present internal random strings in the final design or user-facing output.

## Step 0: Inspect the context

Before proposing a direction:

1. Inspect relevant project files, screenshots, design tokens, brand assets,
   existing components, content, and product documentation.
2. Identify:
   - product and user goal;
   - primary action;
   - target audience;
   - platform and viewport;
   - existing brand or design-system constraints;
   - required content and states;
   - technical, performance, and accessibility constraints.
3. Ask no more than three concise questions, and only when the answers would
   materially alter the direction.
4. When operating autonomously, state reasonable assumptions and continue
   instead of blocking on subjective details.

Do not redesign before understanding what the interface must accomplish.

## Step 1: Frame the aesthetic intent

Write a short internal direction card containing:

- **User job:** what the user is trying to accomplish.
- **Primary action:** the action that must remain visually obvious.
- **Emotional target:** three precise qualities, including one productive tension,
  such as “calm but not sterile” or “playful without becoming childish.”
- **Visual thesis:** one sentence connecting the visual language to the product.
- **Anti-goals:** at least three outcomes the design must avoid.
- **Constraints:** brand, content, accessibility, performance, and platform limits.

Bad thesis:

> Make it modern, clean, and premium.

Better thesis:

> Present complex operational data as a calm editorial field, using strong
> typographic rhythm and one precise signal color so the product feels
> authoritative without resembling an enterprise dashboard.

Do not move forward if the visual thesis could describe almost any product.

## Step 2: Diverge beyond category defaults

Read .

Generate visually and structurally different directions before implementing.

For a page-sized task, produce eight one- or two-sentence directions:

- 3 adjacent but distinctive;
- 3 bold;
- 2 radical or apparently risky.

For larger explorations, expand this to twelve directions.

Requirements:

1. Use at least three different creative operators.
2. Use an external seed for at least one group of directions:
   `python3 scripts/generate-seed.py --count 4`
3. A direction must alter at least four of these dimensions:
   - composition;
   - typography;
   - color logic;
   - material or texture;
   - imagery;
   - motion;
   - interaction;
   - density and rhythm.
4. Do not implement during the first divergence pass.
5. Reject directions that are only adjectives, style labels, or palette swaps.
6. Reject directions that could be predicted from the product category alone.

Score promising directions from 1–5 on:

- relevance to the product;
- distinctiveness;
- internal coherence;
- ownability;
- implementation feasibility.

When interactive selection is appropriate, present the three strongest choices.
When operating autonomously, select the highest-scoring direction and record
a one-sentence rationale.

## Step 3: Define one coherent design language

Use .

The selected direction must define:

- central concept;
- emotional target;
- product or content metaphor;
- one signature device;
- composition rules;
- typography roles;
- palette roles;
- material and imagery treatment;
- interaction and motion principles;
- content voice;
- anti-references;
- accessibility and performance constraints.

Apply the rule:

> One hero move, two supporting moves, and a quiet supporting system.

The signature device should be memorable enough to survive when the logo is
hidden. It may be a compositional rule, type behavior, interaction pattern,
image treatment, material illusion, or spatial metaphor.

Do not make every element expressive. Contrast needs quiet areas.

### References and moodboards

When visual search or reference tools are available:

1. Gather 3–6 references from more than one domain.
2. Include references outside direct competitors where useful.
3. Extract exactly one principle from each reference.
4. Treat references as a quality baseline or moodboard, not a target to copy.
5. Prefer observable principles:
   - hierarchy;
   - cropping;
   - material contrast;
   - scale;
   - pacing;
   - whitespace;
   - interaction rhythm.
6. Avoid combining more than two dominant stylistic influences.

If references are unavailable, proceed from the design thesis and creative
operators rather than inventing fake references.

## Step 4: Build from macro to micro

When implementation is requested:

1. Preserve the existing framework and design system unless replacement is
   explicitly required.
2. Work in this order:
   - information architecture and composition;
   - visual hierarchy;
   - typography;
   - color and material;
   - imagery;
   - interaction and motion;
   - micro-details.
3. Establish or reuse design tokens for:
   - color roles;
   - type roles;
   - spacing rhythm;
   - radius and border rules;
   - elevation;
   - motion duration and easing.
4. Implement the highest-impact composition first.
5. Verify the design at wide and narrow viewports before polishing details.
6. Include relevant loading, empty, error, focus, hover, pressed, and disabled states.
7. Prefer native or established controls when custom controls add no value.

### Generated imagery and video

Use generated media only when it materially advances the design thesis.

Before generating an asset, define:

- its role in the composition;
- subject and framing;
- palette and lighting;
- material treatment;
- consistency requirements;
- crop behavior;
- static or reduced-motion fallback.

Do not use generated media to conceal weak layout or hierarchy.

For video or complex motion:

- use short, optimized, loopable assets where appropriate;
- inspect the result frame by frame;
- prevent layout shift;
- provide a poster or static fallback;
- respect reduced-motion preferences;
- avoid motion that competes with the primary action.

Store credentials only in an ignored local environment file. Never place them
in source code, assets, logs, screenshots, or final output.

### Copy pass

Treat copy as part of the visual system.

- Replace generic claims with product-specific language.
- Remove headings or labels that merely repeat visible information.
- Avoid inflated phrases such as “unlock,” “revolutionize,” “seamless,” or
  “elevate” unless they convey a concrete claim.
- Prefer concise copy with varied sentence rhythm.
- Make calls to action describe the actual next step.

## Step 5: Run an artifact-only critic loop

Read .

When screenshots and subagents are available:

1. Capture screenshots of:
   - a representative wide viewport;
   - a representative narrow viewport;
   - important interactive or content states.
2. Invoke an independent critic in a fresh context.
3. Give the critic:
   - current screenshots;
   - the selected design brief;
   - optional reference images.
4. Do not give the critic:
   - source code;
   - implementation effort;
   - earlier rationales;
   - previous critiques;
   - the desired stopping score.
5. Use the same critic prompt in every round.
6. Ask for:
   - an aesthetic diagnosis;
   - category scores;
   - elements to preserve;
   - the three largest gaps;
   - precise corrective actions;
   - one thing to remove.
7. Implement only the highest-leverage corrections.
8. Capture new screenshots and repeat.

Stop when any condition is met:

- the independent score reaches 9/10;
- four rounds are complete;
- two consecutive rounds improve by less than 0.3;
- further changes would damage clarity, accessibility, or the selected identity.

Keep the 9/10 stopping threshold outside the critic prompt.

If subagents are unavailable, perform one rubric-based self-critique from
screenshots. Explicitly acknowledge the lower independence of that review.

Do not let the critic replace the selected direction with a different style
unless the current concept is fundamentally incompatible with the product.

## Step 6: Subtract and remove generic tells

Read .

For every non-essential element, ask:

1. Does it communicate necessary information?
2. Does it enable an action?
3. Does it clarify state, hierarchy, or navigation?
4. Does it express the selected design identity?

If all answers are no, remove it.

Then perform these tests:

- **Squint test:** hierarchy remains apparent when details are blurred.
- **Logo-off test:** the design retains recognizable character without branding.
- **Category-swap test:** it would not work unchanged for any random competitor.
- **One-memory test:** a viewer can name one distinctive visual idea afterward.
- **Native-control test:** custom controls are genuinely better than platform defaults.
- **Motion-off test:** the static experience still feels complete.
- **Deletion test:** removing decoration improves rather than empties the design.

Generic patterns are not automatically forbidden. Keep them only when they are
conceptually justified, consistently applied, and useful.

## Step 7: Validate and deliver

Before completion:

- run existing build, lint, test, and type-check commands where available;
- verify wide and narrow layouts;
- inspect the browser console;
- test keyboard navigation and visible focus;
- check text contrast and readable line lengths;
- test reduced-motion behavior;
- verify asset dimensions, loading cost, and fallbacks;
- confirm that no credentials or private assets were committed;
- confirm that the primary task remains clear.

### Definition of done

A design is complete when:

- the visual thesis is perceptible within a few seconds;
- the primary action remains clear;
- the design has one memorable and ownable device;
- typography, color, material, imagery, and motion follow the same logic;
- decorative elements have a reason to exist;
- the result works without animation and at narrow widths;
- accessibility and functionality have not regressed;
- the critic threshold or a documented stop condition has been reached.

## Output contract

### Direction mode

Return:

1. assumptions;
2. aesthetic intent;
3. direction slate;
4. scored shortlist;
5. selected direction and rationale;
6. completed design brief;
7. risks and the fastest useful experiment.

### Build or refine mode

Return:

1. selected visual thesis;
2. files or artifacts changed;
3. major design decisions;
4. assets generated or reused;
5. validation performed;
6. critic rounds and scores;
7. remaining risks or compromises.

### Critique mode

Return:

1. aesthetic diagnosis;
2. strengths to preserve;
3. largest gaps;
4. prioritized changes;
5. subtraction opportunities;
6. rubric scores and overall score.

Do not expose internal seed strings unless the user explicitly asks for them.

```markdown
# Creative Operators

Use these operators to escape predictable category defaults.

Randomness creates candidates. Judgment selects candidates.

Do not use creative operators on factual tasks or tasks with only one correct
answer.

## 1. External seed

Generate a seed:

```bash
python3 scripts/generate-seed.py
```

For each direction:

1. Split the string into 5–8 chunks.
2. Assign chunks to design dimensions before interpreting them:
   - composition;
   - typography;
   - color;
   - material;
   - imagery;
   - motion;
   - density.
3. Derive constraints from patterns:
   - repetition may suggest rhythm or modularity;
   - abrupt changes may suggest contrast;
   - sparse patterns may suggest negative space;
   - clusters may suggest zones, sequences, or accent concentration.
4. Let the seed alter at least three design dimensions.
5. Use judgment to reject incoherent results.

The seed is scaffolding, not a user-visible motif or design rationale.

Use a new seed for each independent direction.

## 2. Cross-domain collision

Combine:

- one truth about the product;
- one visual or physical domain;
- one unrelated organizational domain;
- one interaction behavior.

Formula:

> Product truth + domain A + domain B + behavior

Example:

> A financial history presented as geological strata, organized with the
> precision of a museum catalog and revealed through horizontal excavation.

Extract structural principles rather than surface decoration.

Weak collision:

> Make the dashboard look like a spaceship.

Stronger collision:

> Treat live system health as a navigation instrument: stable information sits
> on a fixed horizon while anomalies distort the surrounding field.

## 3. Constraint inversion

List the category defaults, then invert one or two of them.

Possible inversions:

- dense dashboard → selective editorial sequence;
- centered hero → off-axis composition;
- permanent navigation → contextual navigation;
- card grid → continuous field;
- product screenshot → product behavior demonstrated through state changes;
- multiple accent colors → one controlled signal color;
- ornamental animation → motion only at state transitions.

Do not invert every convention simultaneously. Preserve recognizable affordances.

## 4. Productive tension

Pair a desired quality with a boundary:

- luxurious, not ornamental;
- playful, not childish;
- technical, not intimidating;
- expressive, not chaotic;
- minimal, not empty;
- nostalgic, not retro cosplay;
- cinematic, not slow;
- dense, not cluttered.

A good direction resolves the tension rather than choosing only one side.

## 5. Anti-reference

Define what the design must not become.

Use two or three concrete anti-references:

- not a generic enterprise dashboard;
- not a neon cyberpunk control room;
- not a soft beige lifestyle brand;
- not a collection of floating glass cards;
- not a portfolio concept that hides the product task.

Anti-references prevent a promising direction from collapsing into a familiar
template.

## 6. Material translation

Choose a physical material or process and translate its behavior, not merely its
texture.

Consider:

- folding;
- layering;
- abrasion;
- refraction;
- printing;
- embossing;
- weaving;
- cutting;
- casting;
- indexing;
- illumination;
- erosion.

Ask:

- How does the material reveal hierarchy?
- How does it change with state?
- What does it suggest about weight, speed, precision, or trust?
- Can the effect be implemented without harming performance or readability?

## 7. Narrative and state metaphor

Represent product states as meaningful changes in a visual system.

Examples of state transformations:

- assembling and disassembling;
- focusing and defocusing;
- compression and expansion;
- accumulation and erosion;
- alignment and drift;
- signal and noise;
- revealing layers;
- moving from uncertain to resolved.

Prefer metaphors that explain product behavior.

## 8. Scale shift and crop

Change the expected viewing distance:

- extreme macro detail;
- architectural scale;
- cropped fragments;
- miniature systems;
- edge-to-edge content;
- deliberate off-screen continuation.

Scale shifts can create character without adding decoration.

## 9. Rule-breaking budget

Break one major convention deliberately while keeping surrounding conventions
stable.

Possible major breaks:

- unusual reading order;
- extreme asymmetry;
- unconventional navigation position;
- oversized typography;
- controlled color dissonance;
- unusually large negative space.

A broken rule must create meaning, hierarchy, or emotional effect.

Do not break conventions merely to signal creativity.

## Direction rejection filter

Reject a direction if:

- it is only a style adjective;
- it differs only by palette;
- it relies on a gradient, glow, or 3D object as its entire identity;
- it could be used unchanged by most competitors;
- it copies one reference too closely;
- it has no relationship to product behavior or content;
- it obscures the primary action;
- it depends on inaccessible or impractical execution;
- it contains several unrelated “hero” ideas.
```

随机种子适合用于存在多个合理答案的创意任务，而不适合事实检索或单一答案任务；这也符合 String Seed of Thought 研究对适用范围和局限性的说明。

## 四、`references/critic-rubric.md`

```markdown
# Artifact-Only Design Critic

The critic evaluates the visible artifact, not the amount of effort or code
behind it.

Use a fresh context for every critique round.

## Inputs

Provide only:

- current screenshots;
- the intended design brief;
- optional baseline references;
- relevant viewport or state labels.

Do not provide:

- source code;
- implementation history;
- previous critiques;
- developer explanations;
- time spent;
- desired score;
- the stopping threshold.

## Fixed critic prompt

You are an independent design critic evaluating a current visual artifact.

Judge only what is visible in the supplied screenshots. Ignore implementation
effort and do not reward complexity.

First identify the aesthetic and product intent the artifact appears to pursue.
Then imagine how an excellent independent design studio would execute that same
intent. Evaluate the gap between the current artifact and that quality bar.

Treat supplied references as quality baselines, not as designs to copy.

Evaluate both the whole composition and fine details. Penalize generic,
overused, excessive, incoherent, or obviously generated-looking decisions.
Also penalize weak hierarchy, vague copy, poor responsive behavior, misleading
affordances, and decoration that does not support the concept.

Be concise, specific, bold, and actionable. Do not use vague advice such as
“make it cleaner” or “improve hierarchy” without saying exactly what should
change.

Return:

1. AESTHETIC DIAGNOSIS
2. OVERALL SCORE: X/10
3. CATEGORY SCORES
4. PRESERVE: the two strongest decisions
5. TOP GAPS: the three largest gaps, in priority order
6. NEXT ACTIONS: a concrete fix for each gap
7. CUT: one element or pattern to remove
8. RISK: the largest risk if the current direction continues unchanged

Do not propose a completely different style unless the current concept is
fundamentally incompatible with the product goal.

## Scoring dimensions

Score each dimension from 0–10.

### 1. Intent and emotional signal

- Is the intended feeling legible?
- Does it match the product and audience?
- Is it specific rather than generically attractive?

### 2. Distinctiveness and ownability

- Would the design remain recognizable without the logo?
- Does it escape category defaults?
- Is there one memorable device?

### 3. Composition and hierarchy

- Is reading order deliberate?
- Does scale guide attention?
- Is negative space purposeful?
- Is the primary action obvious?

### 4. Typography and content

- Do type roles have clear jobs?
- Are scale, width, spacing, and rhythm controlled?
- Does copy sound specific and human?
- Are line lengths and labels useful?

### 5. Color, material, and imagery

- Do visual materials follow one logic?
- Are accents controlled?
- Does imagery carry meaning?
- Are effects consistent rather than decorative?

### 6. Interaction and motion

- Does motion communicate state or causality?
- Is timing coherent?
- Does the experience remain complete without motion?
- Are interactions discoverable?

### 7. Usability and accessibility

- Is the main task clear?
- Are controls recognizable?
- Are contrast, focus, and responsive behavior sound?
- Is information available without relying only on color or animation?

### 8. Craft and coherence

- Are alignment, spacing, cropping, borders, and states intentional?
- Does every detail belong to the same system?
- Are there unfinished or generated-looking artifacts?

## Score caps

Apply these caps when relevant:

- Broken primary task or misleading controls: overall score ≤ 5.9.
- Serious readability or accessibility failure: overall score ≤ 6.9.
- No memorable device and mostly category defaults: overall score ≤ 7.4.
- Highly polished but derivative or reference-dependent: overall score ≤ 8.4.
- A score of 9 or higher requires distinction, coherence, clarity, and craft;
  visual spectacle alone is insufficient.

The overall score is a holistic judgment, not a mechanical average.

## Implementation-loop guidance

After receiving the critique:

1. Preserve the two strongest decisions.
2. Fix no more than three high-impact issues per round.
3. Prefer structural improvements over decorative additions.
4. Re-capture the same viewports and states.
5. Reuse the exact critic prompt.
6. Do not reveal previous scores to the next critic.
```

## 五、`references/subtraction-checklist.md`

```markdown
# Subtraction and Anti-Generic Checklist

These are diagnostic heuristics, not universal bans.

A familiar pattern may remain when it is justified by the concept, applied
consistently, useful to the user, and compatible with accessibility and
performance.

## Deletion test

For each questionable element, ask:

1. Does removing it lose necessary information?
2. Does removing it lose an action or affordance?
3. Does removing it make state, hierarchy, or navigation unclear?
4. Does removing it damage the design's signature identity?

If every answer is no, remove it.

## Structural tells

Inspect for:

- default navigation followed by a centered or split hero;
- text on the left and interchangeable abstract artwork on the right;
- three equal feature cards regardless of content;
- every section enclosed in a rounded container;
- repetitive icon-heading-description modules;
- excessive pills and badges;
- identical rhythm across every section;
- a marketing template that could accept any company's logo and copy.

Possible corrections:

- organize around the content's real hierarchy;
- vary section rhythm by purpose;
- replace cards with grouping, sequence, comparison, or spatial relationships;
- create one product-specific compositional rule.

## Visual-effect tells

Inspect for:

- unmotivated purple, blue, or rainbow gradients;
- glow used to imply importance;
- glass panels without a material concept;
- floating blobs, rings, grids, or particles;
- excessive shadows and border radii;
- random accent colors;
- a large 3D object functioning only as visual filler;
- image assets with inconsistent lighting, perspective, or style;
- generated imagery containing visible defects.

Possible corrections:

- reduce the palette to functional color roles;
- let typography, imagery, scale, and whitespace carry hierarchy;
- use one effect systematically rather than several effects casually;
- replace decorative imagery with product behavior, real content, or purposeful art.

## Typography tells

Inspect for:

- a generic geometric sans used for every role;
- oversized gradient headlines;
- too many font weights or type scales;
- arbitrary uppercase labels;
- center-aligned long-form copy;
- weak line lengths;
- type that changes style without changing function.

Possible corrections:

- define explicit display, heading, body, label, and data roles;
- create contrast through proportion and rhythm;
- reduce the number of type decisions;
- use alignment that supports reading and content structure.

## Copy tells

Inspect for:

- vague promises;
- repeated explanation of visible content;
- unnecessary section labels;
- interchangeable phrases such as:
  - unlock;
  - elevate;
  - reimagine;
  - seamless;
  - powerful;
  - next-generation;
  - transform your workflow;
- excessive headings followed by very little substance;
- every sentence using the same polished marketing rhythm.

Possible corrections:

- name the actual object, action, result, or constraint;
- delete copy already communicated visually;
- rewrite calls to action as concrete next steps;
- vary sentence length and cadence.

## Motion tells

Inspect for:

- every element fading or sliding into view;
- continuous floating or pulsing;
- animation without a state change;
- identical easing everywhere;
- scroll effects that delay access to content;
- motion that competes with the main action;
- no reduced-motion fallback.

Possible corrections:

- reserve motion for causality, orientation, state, and emphasis;
- reduce the number of animated elements;
- establish a small motion vocabulary;
- make the static frame complete.

## Interaction tells

Inspect for:

- custom controls worse than platform controls;
- hidden navigation introduced only for novelty;
- hover-only information;
- low-contrast or ambiguous buttons;
- icon-only actions without accessible names;
- clickable decoration;
- disabled states that resemble active states.

## Identity tests

### Logo-off test

Temporarily hide the logo and product name.

If the design loses all identity, strengthen its compositional, typographic,
material, or interaction system.

### Category-swap test

Replace the brand and copy with a competitor's.

If nothing feels wrong, the design is insufficiently product-specific.

### One-memory test

After briefly viewing the design, name the single most memorable device.

If several effects compete or nothing is memorable, simplify.

### Squint test

Blur or visually reduce the interface.

The primary hierarchy and action should remain apparent.

### Motion-off test

Disable video and animation.

The static composition should still feel deliberate and complete.
```

## 七、`scripts/generate-seed.py`

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3

"""Generate external random strings for divergent creative exploration."""

from __future__ import annotations

import argparse
import secrets
import string
import sys


ALPHABET = string.ascii_letters + string.digits


def generate_seed(length: int) -> str:
    return "".join(secrets.choice(ALPHABET) for _ in range(length))


def main() -> int:
    parser = argparse.ArgumentParser(
        description=(
            "Generate random alphanumeric strings for creative direction "
            "exploration. Seeds are internal scaffolding and should not be "
            "placed in user-facing designs."
        )
    )
    parser.add_argument(
        "--count",
        type=int,
        default=1,
        help="Number of independent seeds to generate. Default: 1.",
    )
    parser.add_argument(
        "--length",
        type=int,
        default=64,
        help="Length of each seed. Default: 64.",
    )
    args = parser.parse_args()

    if not 1 <= args.count <= 100:
        parser.error("--count must be between 1 and 100")

    if not 16 <= args.length <= 512:
        parser.error("--length must be between 16 and 512")

    for _ in range(args.count):
        print(generate_seed(args.length))

    return 0


if __name__ == "__main__":
    sys.exit(main())
```
